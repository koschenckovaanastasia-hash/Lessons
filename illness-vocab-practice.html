Usage notes

The five places learners usually get this set wrong.

🏠 sick leave — no article

✗ on a sick leave   ✓ on sick leave

He's on sick leave. She went on sick leave in May. He's been off sick all week (more spoken). The paper you bring to work is a sick note / a doctor's note.

🦶 strain vs sprain

strain = overstretch a muscle or tendon — a strained hamstring, I strained my back.

sprain = twist the ligaments of a joint — I sprained my ankle / wrist. For an ankle, native speakers say sprain about ten times more often than strain.

Informal cover-all: I twisted my ankle.

😵 pass out / be out cold / come round

pass out = the moment you lose consciousness (action). He passed out at the sight of blood.

be out cold = the state of being completely unconscious. He was out cold for a minute.

come round (BrE) / come to (AmE) = regain consciousness. She came round in the ambulance.

⚠️ come round also means "visit someone's house" — context decides.

🤒 come down with vs develop

come down with = catch something minor and fast — flu, a cold, a bug. Usually in the continuous: I think I'm coming down with something. Never used with cancer or diabetes.

develop = gradually start to have a condition, neutral and medical: He developed pneumonia / an allergy / arthritis.

🤮 register: vomit / be sick / throw up / puke

vomit — neutral-formal, medical reports. be sick — standard BrE (I was sick twice). throw up — informal, very common. puke / barf — slang, avoid at the doctor's.

Also: swelling is uncountable here (a lot of swelling), a bruise and a rash are countable. You come out in a rash.
